  representation of TFL." US Patent Application US20150074031A1; filed 2013-09-07, published
  2015-03-12, priority 2012-09-11.** **[V]** via Google Patents (abstract read verbatim).

## 3. Q2 — Identity and definite descriptions

**Identity: yes, there is a published treatment, and it is a *reduction*, not an added primitive.**

Verified anchors:
- Sommers, "Do We Need Identity?", *Journal of Philosophy* 66(15):499–504, 1969 (DOI 10.2307/2024565).
  Title alone establishes the question is treated; the curated bibliography annotates it as
  showing "identity definable within syllogistic logic" and introducing the **law of wild
  quantity** — **[P]**.
- Englebretsen, "A Note on Identity, Reference and Logical Form", *Crítica* 13(39):75–81, 1981 **[V]**.
- Englebretsen, "Feldman and Sommers on Leibniz's Law", *Dialogos* 43:91–96, 1984 **[P]** —
  bibliography annotation: shows "Leibniz's law is in fact a principle of term substitutability".
- Englebretsen, "Morris on Identity", *Analysis* 47:92–93, 1987 **[P]**.

The **content** of the reduction, as I could recover it, is:
- `a = b` is read as the conjunction `a is b` **and** `b is a`; reflexivity, symmetry and
  transitivity are then derivable rather than postulated.
- Sortal identity `a is the same φ as b` is reparsed as the categorical `Some φ which is a is b`.

**Both of these statements are [P], and I want to be explicit about how weak the chain is.**
They came to me through (i) a WebSearch summariser's paraphrase of ontology.co/sommersf.htm,
and (ii) a small-model summary of a philosophy blog (Siris/branemrys, Part II of an eight-part
series, 2006). When I fetched ontology.co/sommersf.htm directly, **the identity passage was not
in the returned text** — i.e. the summariser's content did not reproduce on direct fetch. Per
the discipline for this sweep I am **discarding the summariser's wording** and marking the
substantive claim as unconfirmed. What survives verification is only: *there is a published
Sommers treatment of identity in the term-logic setting, dated 1969, plus three Englebretsen
follow-ups.* Anyone building on it must read Sommers 1969 and *Something to Reckon With* Ch. 4.

One corroborating data point that *is* solid: Castro-Manzano et al. 2018 (read in full) states
as a design consequence — "since the syntax of TFL involves the abandon of the Fregean syntax,
TFLPL does not require the use of variables or constants… TFL does need to use the equality
predicate, =, and so in TFLPL we have no need for substitution or unification procedures as in
Prolog." (Verbatim; note the sentence contains an apparent typo — "does need" where the
surrounding argument requires "does *not* need". Quote it only with that flagged.) The
surrounding argument is unambiguous: TFL programs get by without an identity predicate.

**Definite descriptions: I found no dedicated published treatment.** No paper in Sommers'
55-item bibliography, none in Englebretsen's bibliography, and none in Castro-Manzano's corpus
has "description" or "the" in its title or in any abstract I read. The nearest verified
neighbours are Englebretsen's reference/existence cluster (1972, 1974, 1975, 1981) and Ch. 6
"Referential Falls" of *Exploring Topics in the History and Philosophy of Logic* (2015), pp.
99–120, whose contents I could not read. The plausible reconstruction — that "the F" is handled
as a singular term carrying wild quantity, so "the Secretary shall X" transcribes exactly like
"Socrates is mortal" — is **[U]**. It is a reasonable engineering assumption, and it is *not* a
citable claim. If the paper needs to say anything about definite descriptions, it should say
the tradition leaves them to the singular-term apparatus and cite Sommers 1969 / Englebretsen
1980 for that apparatus, without attributing a description theory to anyone.

## 4. Q3 — The algorithmic content of missing-premise suggestion

**Corrected finding, 2026-08-08: the algebraic method is published in the canonical TFL
textbook. The original novelty conclusion below was false because that primary source had
not been read.** See `../missing-premise-priority-audit-2026-08-08.md` for the dedicated
priority audit.

What is verified:
1. Mozes 1989 (DOI 10.1016/S0747-7171(89)80030-6) defines an Aristotelian database by five
   abilities. Two independent Castro-Manzano papers, both read in full, restate them
   near-identically. The 2018 wording, verbatim:
   > "The ability, in answer to yes/no questions, to suggest "missing rules", i.e. rules that if
   > added to the database, will allow proving a "yes" answer."
   The 2021 *Philosophies* wording, verbatim: "when it can suggest implicit rules that, if added
   to the database, could provide affirmative answers".
2. The **invocation protocol** is specified: "if the query is an answer query and it was not
   proven as true or false, the user may ask for implicit rules" (Castro-Manzano et al. 2018,
   verbatim). So it is a user-triggered operation on a *failed* answer query — not automatic.
3. I could **not** read Mozes 1989 itself (ScienceDirect 403). Whether Mozes gives a method or
   just the capability remains unresolved. That question no longer controls priority,
   because Sommers and Englebretsen 2000 supplies the method directly.
4. Sommers and Englebretsen, *An Invitation to Formal Reasoning*, Chapter 5, §3,
   pp. 118–122, was read from a local primary copy on 2026-08-08. On pp. 119–120 the
   authors represent an omitted universal premise as an unknown signed expression, put it
   into the validity equation, and isolate it by subtracting the given premise from the
   conclusion. On pp. 120–121 they reduce a two-unknown case and enumerate the admissible
   premise pairs before selecting the true pair.

The one published, verified instance of premise-supplying in the TFL algebra is narrow:
Castro-Manzano et al. 2018, footnote 16 — "For the valid inferential patterns that need
existential import, like aat-1 or aak-1, the only requirement is to add the missing implicit
premise that states the existence of the minor term, namely, something akin to +S⁰+S⁰."

**Priority verdict:** the proposed `C − Σᵢ Pᵢ` contribution is occupied. The book does not
use the project's free-abelian-group terminology and does not specify the proposed OCaml API,
but it publishes the same essential algorithmic idea: solve the TFL validity equation for
one or more omitted premises. A multiplicity-aware implementation over reusable universals
could still be useful engineering or a narrower formal generalization. It cannot support the
project's broad claim to have invented closed-form or search-free TFL enthymeme completion.
The former Phase 6.1 is therefore cancelled rather than built in search of a shrinking claim.

## 5. Q4 — Adverbial modification

**Finding: nothing. I could not verify any treatment of adverbs or adverbial modification
anywhere in the Sommers/Englebretsen tradition.**

Searched and came up empty: Sommers' complete 55-item bibliography (no adverb/tense/aspect
title); Englebretsen's bibliography on ontology.co; Crossref title+author queries; the chapter
lists of *Something to Reckon With*, *Figuring It Out*, *Exploring Topics…*, and Peterson's
*Intermediate Quantities*; the full text of five Castro-Manzano papers. Multiple targeted
WebSearches returned only general adverb-grammar pages and predicate-functor-logic material,
which is Quine's tradition, not Sommers'.

Two near-misses, both **[U]** and neither an adverb theory:
- Patrick Suppes, "The syntax and semantics of English prepositional phrases", a chapter in
  Oderberg (ed.), *The Old New Logic*, MIT/Bradford 2005 — reported by a search summary, chapter
  list not independently verified. Prepositional phrases, not adverbs, and Suppes' own
  formalism.
- Predicate-functor logic (Quine 1971; Kuhn, *NDJFL* 24(2):233–241, 1983 [P]) uses "functors"
  that permute and duplicate argument places; a search summary glossed these as "adverbs". That
  gloss is about Quine's system and does not transfer to Sommers.

Practical consequence for policy text: "files promptly", "materially breaches", "willfully
fails to" have **no citable term-logic analysis**. The available honest moves are (a) treat the
adverb as part of an atomic relational term (`files-promptly` as one term), which loses the
entailment `files promptly ⊨ files`, or (b) add a meaning postulate `−(files-promptly)+(files)`
as a premise, which recovers monotone-decreasing entailment without any theory. Option (b) is
what the algebra supports; it is an engineering choice we would be originating, and the paper's
limitations section should say so rather than implying tradition backing.

## 6. Q1 — Consolidated list of claimed-but-unimplemented constructions

Ranked by (published-source strength × likely value to policy text):

1. **Murphree's numerical quantifiers** — `−ₙS+P` / `+ₙS+P` / `−ₙS−P` / `+ₙS−P`, plus exact-n,
   fractional, and *numerically quantified relational complexes*. Murphree 1998 [V]; full
   notation and modified decision rule reproduced in Castro-Manzano's CEUR Vol-2585 [V] and its
   ENTCS journal version [V]. **Strict superset of our TFL+ levels** — a/e/i/o and many/most/few
   both fall out as special cases, so this is a generalisation of existing machinery rather than
   a bolt-on. Modified rule, verbatim: validity requires the sum condition, the
   particular-quantity-count condition, and "either (a) the value of a universal conclusion is
   equal to the sum of the values of the universal premises, or (b) the value of a particular
   conclusion is equal to the difference of the universal premise minus the particular."
2. **Modal term logic** — Englebretsen 1988 [V], de re / de dicto distinction with □ and ◊
   attachable to terms *and* to propositions. Directly matches deontic-flavoured policy text
   ("shall", "may", "must not"), though Englebretsen's system is alethic, not deontic, so the
   mapping would be ours to justify.
3. **Free / empty-term term logic** — Castro-Manzano 2020, 2023 [V]. Policy classes are often
   empty ("no applicant under section 4 has…"); existential import is where a certifier gets
   wrong verdicts.
4. **Identity as a first-class relation** — Sommers 1969 [V], reduction content [P]. Needed for
   "the applicant named in section 4 is the person who filed".
5. **Relevance-filtered validity** — Castro-Manzano 2022 [V], 2024 [V]. TFL proves some things a
   reader would call irrelevant; the tradition has a published fix at the tableaux level.
6. **Peterson's five-quantity compleat syllogistic and fractional quantifiers** — Peterson &
   Carnes [V] (chapter titles verified, contents not read).
7. **Statistical / probabilistic syllogistic** — Castro-Manzano 2021 [V].
8. **Non-monotonic inference** — Castro-Manzano 2017 [V], diagrammatic form only.
9. **Disjunctive/implicational compound terms**, if we lack them — Massie patent [V] treats
   ∩, ∪, ⊃ as the compound-term repertoire.
10. **Mozes' interval-valued relation arguments** (`age(X,0-120)`) [V via restatement].
11. **Cross-sentence anaphora** — Purdy 1994 [V], but in a different formalism; a port, not an
    extension.
